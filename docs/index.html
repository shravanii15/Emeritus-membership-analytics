The problem

A university's emeritus (retired-faculty) association kept its member roster, dues payments, and subscription records in three separate spreadsheets maintained by different people over several years. There was no shared ID between them — just names, with all the usual real-world mess: typos, nicknames, people who moved, duplicate entries.

Nobody could reliably answer "who's actually an active member right now?" — let alone "who's at risk of not renewing?" or "are we spending money on benefits people don't use?"

This project rebuilds that into one clean, linked dataset, then uses it to predict renewal risk, segment members for more relevant outreach, optimize a volunteer phone campaign under a real time constraint, and test a specific budget question: should out-of-state members keep getting campus parking passes they're unlikely to use?

What I built

Five pieces, each solving one part of the problem — all documented and tested in the repo.

🧹

Record linkage

A two-tier fuzzy matching strategy (ID first, normalized name+zip second) reconciles the three files into one trustworthy member record.

🤖

Renewal prediction

Three models compared with cross-validation and bootstrap confidence intervals, plus SHAP explanations for every prediction.

🧩

Member segmentation

K-Means groups members into behaviorally distinct types, so outreach messaging isn't one-size-fits-all.

📞

Outreach optimizer

A budget-constrained optimizer decides who a limited volunteer phone team should call to recover the most renewals per hour.

🅿️

Budget-impact analysis

Estimates the savings from not issuing parking passes to out-of-state members — shown as a range, not a false-precision number.

🔬

Methods research

A controlled experiment testing how record-linkage accuracy degrades under data-entry noise — the one fully falsifiable finding.

Case study

Problem → constraints → decisions → results, the way I'd walk through it in an interview.

Problem
Years of fragmented membership and dues spreadsheets meant the association had no reliable, unified view of who was an active member — let alone whether a recurring cost like parking passes was being allocated well.
Constraints
The underlying data is sensitive (names, addresses, payment records), and the people who needed the answer — membership-office staff — are not technical users. Any tool had to protect privacy by design and be usable without a data background.
Decisions
Built a two-tier record-linkage strategy instead of requiring a shared ID that didn't exist. Published this version on synthetic data so the method and code can be shared publicly. Framed every dollar estimate as a sensitivity range rather than a single confident number, because the source data can't fully confirm usage.

Honest limitations

What this project doesn't claim

  • All data is 100% synthetic — the patterns the model finds are patterns built into the generator, not real-world discoveries. The linkage robustness experiment (on the live dashboard, linked above) is the one exception: it's a claim about the method, independent of the data being fake.
  • The parking-pass savings estimate rests on two unmeasured assumptions (per-pass cost, actual usage rate) — it's shown as a range, and its real recommendation is "audit this," not the dollar figure itself.
  • The outreach "call lift" curve is an assumed behavioral model, not fit from a randomized experiment.

All data on this site is synthetically generated and does not represent any real person.
